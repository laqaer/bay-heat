GFCI: when it applies and when it doesn't

A code or reference citationNFPA 70, National Electrical Code, 2023 · checked 2026-09-25Report a problem covers receptacles — outlets you plug something into. A hardwired 240V heater has no plug, so this rule doesn't attach to the heater itself. It still governs any 125–250V receptacle elsewhere in the garage, so an electrician wiring a new circuit for the heater will usually address both in the same visit. Your electrician and your local code edition govern.

Safety

A hardwired 240V heater is a fixed continuous load (A code or reference citationNFPA 70, National Electrical Code, 2023 · checked 2026-09-25Report a problem), sized at 125% of its running current — that's where every breaker and wire figure on this page comes from. It isn't a receptacle, so the GFCI rule (A code or reference citationNFPA 70, National Electrical Code, 2023 · checked 2026-09-25Report a problem) governs the garage's outlets, not the heater. Questions

What breaker and wire does a 5,000 W 240 V garage heater need?

A 5,000 W 240 V heater needs a 30 A breaker and 10 AWG copper. It draws 20.8 A while it runs. The breaker is larger than that running current because a fixed electric heater is a continuous load.

Will a 4,000 W 240 V heater run on a 20 A circuit?

No. A 4,000 W heater draws 16.7 A, and the continuous-load rule sizes the breaker at 125% of that current. It needs its own 25 A circuit and 10 AWG copper. It does not fit the 20 A circuit already run to most garages.

What breaker and wire do 7,500 W and 10,000 W heaters need?

A 7,500 W heater needs a 40 A breaker and 8 AWG copper. A 10,000 W heater needs a 60 A breaker and 4 AWG copper.

Does a hardwired 240 V garage heater need GFCI?

NEC 2023 requires GFCI on garage receptacles, the outlets you plug into. A hardwired 240 V heater has no plug, so that rule does not attach to the heater itself. It still covers other receptacles in the garage. Your electrician and your local code edition govern.
