By size Start with square footage and insulation. A 1,500 W plug-in unit is a workbench heater, not a two-car garage heater. -> /best-electric-garage-heaters-by-size
120V vs 240V Voltage is not a feature. It is the limit of the circuit you already have — or the one you are willing to install. -> /120v-vs-240v-garage-heater
Forced-air vs IR Forced-air heats the air. Infrared heats people and objects. Drafts punish the first; open-door work favors the second. -> /forced-air-vs-infrared-garage-heater
Ceiling under $200 Most sub-$200 ceiling units are the same job: 5 kW, 240 V, hardwired, 30 A, 10 AWG copper. The differences are controls and jumpers, not magic BTUs. -> /best-ceiling-mount-garage-heaters-under-200
Wall vs ceiling Ceiling units throw across the bay if the joists can take 25–30 lb. Wall mounts win on low ceilings and one work zone. The manual’s clearance is the rule, not a blog height. -> /wall-mount-vs-ceiling-garage-heater
Insulate first More watts heat the air you are already leaking. Door, weatherstrip, and sill usually beat a larger breaker. Panel capacity is the hard stop. -> /insulate-garage-before-heater-upgrade
Cost to run Cost is nameplate watts, hours the element is actually on, and the rate on your bill. A 1,500 W portable is cheap per hour because it is spot heat, not because 120 V is efficient. -> /electric-garage-heater-operating-cost
Electric vs propane Electric wins on enclosed, occupied-adjacent air. Propane can win BTU and $/BTU in a drafty detached shop if it is listed and vented for that job. -> /electric-vs-propane-garage-heater
